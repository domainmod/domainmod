As of v4.27.0, the Watch List keeps track of domains you don't own yet, such as ones you'd like to buy or catch when they drop. DomainMOD checks each one in the background with public RDAP data, or WHOIS for TLDs without RDAP, and shows its expiry date, registrar, registry statuses, and stage, with an estimated drop date for generic TLDs.

Requirement

The checks are run by the Check Watch List task, so you must set up the cron job that comes with DomainMOD (Docker installs already run it), and keep the task enabled in the Task Scheduler.

Adding Domains

  1. Click Watch List on the main menu.

  2. Click the Add Domains button.

  3. Paste in the domains you want to watch, one per line.

  4. Choose a Reason (Want to buy, Backorder placed, Try to catch when it drops, Negotiating, or Just watching), and add any Notes. Both are added to every domain on the list.

  5. Click the Add Domains button.

The Watch List holds up to 1,000 domains, and archived domains don't count toward the limit. Domains you already have in DomainMOD can't be watched, unless they're Expired or Sold. Checks begin on the task's next scheduled run, which works through the list a few domains at a time.

Stages

Each domain's Stage comes from its latest check:

  • Not checked yet, Checking, Checking again, or Unable to check: the domain hasn't been checked yet, a new result is being confirmed (see below), or the lookups have failed so far
  • Registered or Newly registered: someone owns the domain
  • Expiry date passed: the published expiry date has passed, but the registry hasn't reported a grace period
  • Expired, in grace period: the registry reports the domain as expired, and the owner can usually still renew it
  • In redemption: the owner can usually still restore the domain
  • Restore pending: the owner has asked the registry to restore the domain
  • Pending delete: the domain is about to be deleted, and usually can't be restored
  • Not registered: the registry returned no record for the domain. It may be available, or it may be reserved, blocked, or offered at a premium price, so check with a registrar.

A change into or out of Not registered is only recorded once a second lookup, about 15 minutes later, agrees with the first. Until then, the stage is Checking (for a new domain) or Checking again, and the domain's edit page says when the second lookup is due. The exception is a newly added or unarchived domain whose first RDAP answer says it isn't registered, which shows as Not registered straight away.

How Often Domains Are Checked

Registered domains are checked weekly while they're far from expiry, and daily in the last 60 days before they expire. Domains in the other stages are checked daily, and up to 50 domains that are close to dropping are checked every few hours. Failed checks are tried again less often each time, and a domain whose RDAP or WHOIS server is busy or limiting requests waits an hour.

The Drop Estimate

For generic TLDs (such as .com, .net, and .org), the Estimated Drop column shows the range of dates when the domain is likely to be deleted and become available, based on its statuses and ICANN's usual rules: up to 45 days of grace period, 30 days of redemption, and 5 days of pending delete. A Rough estimate is worked out during the grace period from the expiry date seen before it (so a domain first seen in grace has none), an Estimate from when redemption was first seen, and Likely from when pending delete was first seen. Other TLDs have their own rules, so their stage and statuses are still checked, but no drop date is estimated.

Note

Drop dates are only estimates, based on the domain's statuses and the TLD's usual rules. The owner can usually renew during the grace period and restore during redemption, so the domain may never drop. Once deletion is pending, it usually can't be restored. Popular names are often caught by paid backorder services the moment they drop, or never become available to register at all. A watched domain's page shows a similar note beside its estimate.

The Watch List Page

The list shows each domain's Stage, Expiry date, Registered date, Estimated Drop, Registrar, Reason, Added By, Last Checked, and Next Check. "(not updated)" beside a Last Checked time means the latest check didn't get an answer, so the details are from an earlier one.

You can filter the list by Stage, Reason, TLD, Estimated Drop (Next 7 days, Next 30 days, or Any estimate), Added By, and whether it's Archived. To change several domains at once, tick them, choose Archive, Unarchive, Change the reason, or Delete from the With the selected domains menu, then click Apply. Export downloads the list as a CSV file, and Changes opens the watched domains' Change History.

A Watched Domain's Page

Click a domain to open its page, where you can:

  • change its Reason and Notes
  • see its current state, including its registry statuses and registration date
  • click Check Now to look it up straight away (within 10 minutes of the last check, it shows that check's details instead)
  • Archive it, which stops the checks and keeps the entry and its history, or Unarchive it, which starts watching it again from scratch
  • Delete it (its earlier changes stay in the Change History)
  • view its public RDAP record

I Own This Now

Once you've bought a watched domain, the I Own This Now section on its page opens a form to add it to your domains with its name filled in, using either Import with RDAP (recommended), which adds it through the Domain Queue, or Add Manually. Once the domain is in your domains and isn't Expired or Sold, DomainMOD archives the watched domain automatically. If the domain is already in DomainMOD as Expired or Sold, set its Domain Status to Active and save instead, then use Refresh to update its details.

Turning the Watch List Off

The Watch List is on by default. If you don't use it, an administrator can turn off Use the Watch List in Administration -> System Settings, under Watch List, then click Update Watch List Setting. This hides it from every user and stops its checks. Your watched domains are kept, so turning it back on brings everything back.
